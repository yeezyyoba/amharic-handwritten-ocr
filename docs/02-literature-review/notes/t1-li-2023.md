# TrOCR: Transformer-Based Optical Character Recognition with Pre-trained Models (Li et al., 2023)

**Citation (IEEE):** M. Li *et al.*, "TrOCR: Transformer-based optical character recognition with pre-trained models," in *Proc. AAAI Conf. Artificial Intelligence*, vol. 37, 2023, pp. 13094–13102.
**Link / DOI:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10282 

## Problem

Text recognition systems usually use a CNN to understand the image and an RNN with CTC to generate the text, and then add a separate language model to improve accuracy. Their parameters are trained from scratch, so large pre-trained image and language models are left unused. The paper asks whether pre-trained image and text Transformers can replace the CNN and the RNN in one end-to-end model.

## Method

- **Encoder:** an image Transformer (ViT-style, initialised from DeiT or BEiT). The input line is resized to 384 × 384 and cut into 16 × 16 patches. 
- **Decoder:** a standard Transformer decoder initialised from RoBERTa or MiniLM, generating wordpiece tokens (BPE and SentencePiece) with beam search (beam size 10). 
- **Pre-training in two stages:** stage one on 684 million synthetic printed text lines (from about 2 million PDF pages). Stage two on task-specific data: 17.9 million synthetic handwritten lines (5,427 handwriting fonts), 3.3 million printed lines, and about 16 million scene-text images. Pre-training used 32 V100 GPUs. 
- **Fine-tuning** with data augmentation (rotation, blur, dilation, erosion, downscaling, underlining). 
- Three sizes: TrOCR-small (62M parameters), base (334M), large (558M). 
## Data

SROIE task 2 (printed receipts, word-level F1), IAM Handwriting Database (Aachen partition: 6,161 training, 966 validation and 2,915 test lines, CER), and scene-text benchmarks (word accuracy). 

## Evaluation

SROIE: word-level precision, recall and F1. IAM: case-sensitive CER. Scene text: word accuracy over a 36-character set. The baseline named for CRNN is a public PyTorch implementation initialised from its provided pre-trained model.

## Strengths

- Convolution-free and needs no separate language model or complex post-processing.
- A real ablation of pre-trained initialisation, augmentation and both pre-training stages.
- Tests printed, handwritten and scene text, and releases models and code.
- The small model is an order of magnitude smaller and about twice as fast as the large one.

## Weaknesses

- Pre-training at the scale of 684 million lines is out of reach for a low-resource script.
- The CRNN baseline is not tuned for these tasks (F1 36.09 on SROIE), so it is weak evidence about CTC models in general. In the paper's own IAM table the CTC model of Diaz et al. with a language model (2.75) beats TrOCR-large (2.89).
- Only TrOCR-large beats the best SROIE leaderboard entry (96.58 against 96.43). TrOCR-base (96.34) and small (95.82) do not.
- Only TrOCR-large (2.89) beats the CTC system of Bluche and Messina (3.2). The paper never runs an external language model on TrOCR itself.
- SROIE is printed receipts, and the IAM split sizes differ from the split Kang et al. describe (6,482 / 976 / 2,914 lines), so IAM figures across the two papers may not be directly comparable.
- The claim that the model extends to other languages by changing the decoder and dictionary is stated but not tested.
- No variance or repeated runs are reported.

## Relevance to our project

- Compute is the constraint stated in the proposal. TrOCR-small (62M, 8.37 sentences/s) is the realistic size for our resources.
- The 36.09 F1 CRNN result should not be cited as evidence that CRNN is worse than TrOCR. Our own comparison under identical splits and preprocessing is the way to answer that.
- Table 4 shows the CTC and Transformer ranking changes when an external language model is added, which is the reason to evaluate the correction module separately on each model.
